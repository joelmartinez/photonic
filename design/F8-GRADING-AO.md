# F8 — grading and ambient occlusion as an engine output step

This record covers roadmap item F8 (2026-11-12). It says what the engine's output step now does, what the defaults are, and how each piece was verified, including the one place where the first measurement misled and what a control experiment showed about it.

## What was wrong

The renderer drew the scene directly: no tone mapping, so bright colors clipped to flat saturated patches, no post-processing of any kind, so contact areas had no depth, and the pixel ratio followed the device with no cap, so high-density displays paid full price for sharpness past the visible gain. All three gaps were measured in the codecube.net integration project's Q1 record, which filed this request; the engine was the right home for the fix because every consumer shares the renderer.

## What the engine does now

The work sits in src/PhotonicRenderer.js, which owns the output step, with a thin set of controls on PhotonicScene that delegate to it. Grading is on by default: the renderer runs ACES filmic tone mapping, and the exposure is a plain control through setExposure and getExposure on both the renderer and the scene, or as an exposure option at construction; setToneMapping accepts the friendly names none, linear, reinhard, cineon, aces, agx, and neutral (or a raw THREE constant), so a scene that needs the old ungraded look can still ask for it. The pixel ratio is capped at 2 by default, the cap itself is a control through setPixelRatioCap, and changing it re-applies the ratio to the renderer and to the composer when one exists.

Ambient occlusion is an optional composer chain, off by default because it costs frames: enablePostProcessing on the scene (or postProcessing: true, or an options object, at construction) routes rendering through an EffectComposer holding a render pass, a GTAOPass from the Three.js r170 addons, and an output pass. The output pass is what applies tone mapping and color-space conversion at the end of the chain, so a scene looks graded the same way whether the chain is on or off; with the chain off the renderer applies the same ACES curve directly. GTAO was chosen over SSAO because it works from the scene's normals and depth with a denoise step, which holds up better at the meter scale Photonic scenes use. The pass is tuned through setPostProcessingOptions (blendIntensity for strength, plus GTAO's radius, distanceExponent, thickness, scale, and samples), and getPostProcessingInfo reports the whole output state: whether the chain is enabled and active, the exposure, the tone mapping mode, and the pixel ratio against its cap. The chain steps aside on its own while a WebXR session is presenting, because the composer is a flat-screen chain and XR renders through the direct path, grading included.

One integration note matters for consumers: the composer classes are imported by the renderer from three/addons/, the same specifier family PhotonicCamera already uses for its orbit controls, and the README quickstart import map already resolves it, so the standalone-consumption shape does not change; the package entry still loads in Node with the same 28 exports.

## Verification

Everything below ran headlessly in Chromium with software rendering (SwiftShader), serving this repo's own pages, and the captures live in design/evidence/.

Grading and the controls check out by state and by pixels: a scene reports tone mapping aces with the renderer's constant at the ACES filmic value, exposure defaults to 1, dropping exposure to 0.45 drops the box capture's mean luminance from 140.6 to 97.9, and a scene built with construction options (postProcessing: true, exposure 1.2, pixel ratio cap 1.5) reports exactly those values with the chain active. The pixel-ratio cap was checked on an emulated 3x display, where the renderer's ratio reads 2, not 3.

The required example captures are design/evidence/f8-box-off.png and f8-box-on.png and the f8-solar-off.png and f8-solar-on.png pair, the real box and solar-system example modules loaded into a PhotonicScene with the chain off and on. Both scenes render cleanly both ways with no page errors, and the shadows from the shadows work are present in all four. With samples taken on visible floor right beside the objects, the occlusion measures out in both: floor beside the box reads about 85 with the chain off and about 80 with it on, the sun's contact zone in the solar-system scene drops from 87.4 to 57.4, and open floor away from any object is unchanged (145.8 against 145.9), so the darkening sits exactly where contact is and nowhere else.

The occlusion is plainly visible where surfaces actually meet: design/evidence/f8-ao-probe-off.png and f8-ao-probe-on.png show a corner built from the engine's own plane, box, and sphere creators, and with the chain on the wall-to-floor seams, the corner behind the box, and the ground under the sphere all gain a soft dark contact band that the off capture does not have; a sample of the floor in the box's wall gap drops from 121.7 to 115.9 while open floor stays at 138 in both.

## Why the examples show so little occlusion, and how that was pinned down

The first box measurements looked like a defect: with the chain on, the floor beside the box barely changed. That reading turned out to be two things at once, and both are worth keeping here. The first was a sampling mistake of the same kind the shadows work recorded: the point chosen for "floor beside the box" projected through the box itself, so it read the box's shaded face in both captures; moving the samples to visible floor on the camera side of the box produced the drop reported above. The second is real scene character, separated from engine trouble by three checks: the GTAO pass's own buffers were captured directly (the normal buffer is correct, floor normals up and box faces right, and the raw occlusion buffer is white except for a line exactly at the box's base, so the pass runs and finds the contact that is there); a control page with no Photonic code at all — raw Three.js, the textbook composer chain, a box on a plane — produced the same near-white occlusion buffer and the same faint composite, at the default radius and at six times the radius; and the corner probe above, where walls give the horizon search something to work with, shows the effect strongly through the same engine path. So a lone object on an open floor seen at a grazing angle is close to GTAO's weakest case, and that weakness is shared with stock Three.js rather than caused by Photonic's wiring. Scenes with corners, furniture, or close objects — the liminal-style interiors this step was filed for — are where the chain earns its cost, and consumers can raise blendIntensity or radius per scene when they want it stronger.

## Frame cost

Measured in the same headless runs, averaged over 40 frames at 820x640 after a warmup: the box scene costs 121.5 ms per frame direct and 1732.1 ms per frame through the chain; the solar-system scene costs 127.7 ms direct and 1665.3 ms through the chain. A repeat run landed within a few percent of both pairs. Those are software-rendering numbers and they flatter neither path: SwiftShader runs the GTAO shader's full-screen passes on a CPU, where they are disproportionately expensive, so the roughly fourteen-times multiple is a property of the test rig, not a prediction for a real GPU, where this chain at this size is a few milliseconds. What the numbers do establish honestly is the ordering and the reason the chain is opt-in: ambient occlusion is the expensive part of the output step, grading and the pixel cap are free by comparison (the graded direct path costs the same as the old ungraded one within noise), and a scene turns the chain on only when its content benefits.

## Regression checks

The library entry still imports in Node with all 28 exports, and the standalone host page still loads the box module through the scene loader with no page errors, so the way consumers load the package is unchanged. Nothing was pushed to GitHub and nothing was published to npm; the mirror snapshot follows the usual interactive route after a roadmap-level stage, and when it lands, codecube.net can delete the temporary site-side grading switches its Q1 work is carrying.
