# Photonic — 3D skeleton and the not-seen bone style (F3, 2026-11-11)

PhotonicSkeleton, in src/PhotonicSkeleton.js and exported from the library entry, draws a pose skeleton inside a 3D scene: it takes a PhotonicScene, a THREE scene, or a THREE group in its constructor, adds one group of its own, and draws each pose handed to setPose in plain world units, with no coordinate conversion, because a 3D consumer's joints already live in the scene's space. The look is the KataPose MVP look the overlay uses, carried into 3D unchanged: seen bones are translucent outlined cylinders, seen joints are glowing spheres, all of it in the cyan-blue scheme with simple geometry and every value behind the look in DEFAULT_SKELETON_STYLE, overridable through the constructor or setStyle.

The reason this class exists is the not-seen style (roadmap F3, filed by the KataPose Lab): Photonic had no dashed style, so a consumer showing a skeleton in 3D could only fake an unmeasured bone with faint opacity, and a faint cylinder still reads as a thin measured bone. Here, a joint counts as seen at or above the style's confidence threshold, which defaults to 0.5 because that is the seen threshold the KataPose capture and grading pipeline uses everywhere, and a bone whose endpoints are both present but not both seen is drawn as a dashed line in a neutral gray instead of a cylinder, with the unmeasured joints themselves shown as hollow wireframe spheres in the same gray, matching the overlay's hollow marker. A bone touching a joint that is absent from the pose entirely is hidden rather than dashed, because there is no position to draw it through, and both behaviors can be turned off through the showNotSeenBones and showHollowJoints flags, the constructor or setPose options, or their setters, for a consumer that wants the older plain look. getBoneState reports each connection as seen, dashed, or absent, getJointState does the same per joint as seen, hollow, or absent, and getCounts separates measured joints and bones from hollow joints and dashed bones, which is what the verification below asserts.

Poses are accepted in the shapes pose estimators already produce: a name-keyed object, an array in the order of the jointNames option (the COCO 17 names by default), a joints or keypoints wrapper, or the Map that normalizeSkeletonPose returns, with each joint carrying its world position plus a confidence, visibility, or explicit seen flag, and a consumer with a different skeleton passes its own connection pairs and joint names to the constructor.

The runnable proof is examples/skeleton-demo/, a PhotonicScene holding one standing figure whose right knee and right ankle carry low confidence, so that leg renders as two hollow joints joined by dashed bones while the rest of the body stays solid, with checkboxes that toggle the dashed bones and the hollow markers; opening it with ?still=1 freezes the pose for screenshots and tests. The verification performed for F3, including the headless checks on counts, bone states, and the dashed line material, is recorded in PROGRESS.md.

One honest limit: the dashed line is a one-pixel line in most WebGL renderers, so it reads as a fine dotted trace rather than a thick dashed tube; that thinness is part of why it cannot be mistaken for a measured bone, and a consumer that needs a heavier mark can raise the line's opacity or color contrast through the style, but not its width, which the platform does not support.
