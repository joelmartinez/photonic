# F12 — physical materials and an image-based environment for adopted geometry

This record covers roadmap item F12 (2026-11-14), filed by the HootCAD integration session as the named remainder of item F-2 in the HootCAD rendering test space. It says what was missing, what the public API is, and how each claim was verified, including the limits.

## What was wrong

The test space's F-2 asks for metalness, roughness, and clearcoat that read correctly under an environment, proven on meshes adopted through F10. Two of the three were already covered: addGeometry builds a MeshStandardMaterial with caller roughness and metalness, and addMesh honours a caller-built material. Clearcoat was covered nowhere: it lives on Three's MeshPhysicalMaterial, and no Photonic material path offered that type — not the material factory, not the created shapes, not adopted geometry. And there was no environment to read any of it under: the engine never set scene.environment and shipped no environment or PMREM helper, so a metal part was lit only by the direct lights. That second gap is the visible one: a metal surface reflects its surroundings, and with no surroundings a machined part at metalness 0.9 renders nearly black except where a light happens to strike it, whatever its roughness says. The before capture below is that failure, measured: the part's sampled faces average a luminance of about 21 out of 255.

## The physical material surface

One option shape runs across all three material paths, so a caller learns it once: physical: true (or materialType: 'physical' on the geometry calls, or type: 'physical' on the factory), clearcoat, and clearcoatRoughness, with envMapIntensity carried alongside. Asking for clearcoat by itself also selects the physical material, because clearcoat exists on no other type and silently dropping it would be the worst outcome. When a physical material is built and no clearcoat value is given, the coat defaults to fully on (clearcoat 1, clearcoatRoughness 0.12): asking for physical in Photonic means asking for the clearcoat look this item exists to provide, and clearcoat: 0 opts out of the coat while keeping the physical type.

scene.createMaterial is the factory path, and the one created objects use through their material option: type: 'physical' returns a MeshPhysicalMaterial with the clearcoat pair set from the options, and the standard path upgrades to the same type when a clearcoat value rides along; plain standard requests are untouched, and a per-material envMapIntensity passes through on both standard and physical results. Clearcoat maps (clearcoatMap, clearcoatRoughnessMap, clearcoatNormalMap) pass through when supplied.

scene.addGeometry keeps building its own default material when the caller supplies none, and that material is physical when the call asks for it, with the call's colour, roughness, metalness, vertex colours, and side carried exactly as the standard default carries them. A caller-supplied material is still honoured as-is.

scene.addMesh had the subtlest case, because the adopted mesh arrives wearing the caller's own material. When the adoption options ask for physical and supply no material of their own, every mesh inside the adopted object has its material given the physical surface: a material that is already physical is tuned in place, and any other material is replaced by a MeshPhysicalMaterial that copies its colour, roughness, metalness, emissive, maps, opacity, transparency, side, vertex-colour flag, and env intensity, then applies the clearcoat pair. The replaced material is left undisposed, because its owner is the caller, who may still hold it for other meshes. When the adoption options do supply a material, that material wins exactly as before and no upgrade happens.

## The environment helper

The new src/PhotonicEnvironment.js exports createStudioEnvironment(renderer, options), which builds Three's RoomEnvironment — the neutral studio room of soft area lights the Three examples use for exactly this purpose — through a PMREMGenerator and returns the resulting texture with a dispose. It is a small, honest helper: one fixed neutral environment, no HDR files, no network fetch, nothing to configure beyond the PMREM blur sigma.

PhotonicScene carries the surface a consumer actually calls. scene.setStudioEnvironment({ intensity, background }) builds the studio environment and applies it as scene.environment; scene.setEnvironment(texture, options) adopts a caller-supplied environment texture the same way, for consumers who build their own PMREM or load an HDR; scene.setEnvironmentIntensity and scene.getEnvironmentIntensity drive scene.environmentIntensity, which Three applies to every material's environment response at once, and scene.getEnvironmentInfo reports whether an environment is on, where it came from ('studio' or 'custom'), and its intensity. The background option also shows the environment as the scene background, and it is off by default, because an environment's first job is light, not backdrop. scene.clearEnvironment removes the environment, restores the default background when the environment was supplying it, and disposes the studio texture the scene built; a caller-supplied texture is not disposed, because the caller owns it. The studio environment can also be requested at construction, as new PhotonicScene(container, { environment: true }) or with an options object, mirroring the F8 post-processing option, and scene.dispose clears it with the rest of the scene.

## Verification

Everything below ran headlessly in Chromium with software rendering against the new examples/physical-materials-demo/, which builds its part the way JSCAD data arrives — a fan-triangulated polygon soup for a plate, a boss with a counterbore, and a step pad — and adopts it through addGeometry as one metal material (metalness 0.9, roughness 0.32), beside a caller-built red sphere adopted through addMesh. The page's before mode builds both with plain standard materials and no environment; its after mode builds the part with the physical option (clearcoat 1, clearcoat roughness 0.08) and the knob with the addMesh physical upgrade (clearcoat 1, roughness 0.06), under the studio environment at intensity 1. Twenty-six checks pass, with no page errors on either page and none on the standalone host page regression run: the before state is standard throughout with no environment set; the after state is physical on both adopted objects with the clearcoat pairs exact, the part's metalness and roughness preserved, and the knob's colour (0x9e1b1b) and roughness (0.35) preserved through its upgrade; the studio environment is set with scene.environmentIntensity at 1 and reported by getEnvironmentInfo as studio; the factory returns physical for the physical type and for a clearcoat-carrying standard request, coat-on defaults when no coat value is given, standard for a plain request, and a coat-free physical for clearcoat: 0; the intensity setter reaches scene.environmentIntensity; clearing reports the environment off and rebuilding reapplies it at a new intensity; and a scene constructed with the environment option starts with the studio applied at the requested intensity.

The pixel evidence is the point of the item, sampled from the rendered canvas at the same projected points in both modes. The metal part's three sampled faces (boss top, plate top, step pad) average a luminance of 20.7 before and 176.5 after: the near-black metal of the filing is gone, and the part reads as bright machined stock with its bore and pad legible. The painted knob averages 39.7 before and 137.8 after, and the after capture (design/evidence/f12-part-physical-env.png, with f12-part-before.png beside it) shows why: the knob carries several sharp studio reflections across its clearcoat, the classic car-paint read, where the before capture shows one dull highlight. That the environment, not some other change, does the work is proven by the intensity control: dimming it to 0.15 with everything else held drops the part's sampled average to 64.6. Both captures were read back directly and show the states their status lines claim.

Regression checks: the library entry imports in Node with all 29 exports (createStudioEnvironment is the one new export; the rest of the surface is new scene methods and options), a strict TypeScript 5 consumer using the factory's physical options, both geometry calls with clearcoat options, the environment methods, and the exported helper compiles clean against src/index.d.ts, and npm pack --dry-run lists 52 files including src/PhotonicEnvironment.js, the new demo, and this record.

## Limits

The studio environment is the one neutral room; a consumer who needs a specific HDRI loads it themselves and hands the texture to setEnvironment, which is why that call exists. The physical surface is deliberately the clearcoat subset the request named — clearcoat, its roughness, and its maps — plus env intensity; the rest of MeshPhysicalMaterial (transmission, sheen, iridescence) is not wrapped, and a caller who needs it builds the material with Three directly and hands it to addMesh or a creator's material option, which the engine already honours. Upgrading an adopted material replaces it rather than mutating it (except one already physical), so a material shared with meshes outside the adopted object keeps its standard form there; the demo's knob is the single-owner case. Nothing was pushed to GitHub and nothing was published to npm; the release decision is Joel's, and the docs site's vendored library copy refreshes through docs/refresh-vendored.mjs when the release is cut, as it did for 0.2.0 through 0.4.0.
